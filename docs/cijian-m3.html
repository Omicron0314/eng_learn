The Last Winter at Gull Point

For thirty years, Elias had kept the lamp burning at Gull Point. Every evening he climbed the narrow stairs, wiped the salt from the glass, and watched the beam meander across the dark water.

When the letter arrived in November, he read it twice. The lighthouse would be automated by spring. He was not angry, only reluctant to imagine the tower without footsteps, without the small, vivid rituals that had shaped his days.

His daughter called that night. “You could finally rest,” she said. Elias looked at the scaffold the workers had left against the wall and smiled. “Maybe,” he answered. “But someone should say goodbye to the light.”
